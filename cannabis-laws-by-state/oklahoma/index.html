Oklahoma Cannabis Laws

The basics from the Oklahoma Medical Marijuana Authority (OMMA)’s official pages, checked October 27, 2026. General information, not legal advice.

Official state site

Oklahoma Medical Marijuana Authority (OMMA)

The rules in Oklahoma

General information, not legal advice. Each statement on this page was checked against the official state page it links to on October 27, 2026. Rules change: check the official source before you rely on it.

Legal status

  1. 01

    Marijuana is legal for medical use in Oklahoma.

    Source: oklahoma.gov (official source, opens in a new tab) retrieved October 27, 2026

Age

  1. 02

    Oklahoma residents aged 18 and up are eligible for an adult patient license from the Oklahoma Medical Marijuana Authority (OMMA); residents aged 17 and younger are eligible for a minor patient license.

    Source: oklahoma.gov (official source, opens in a new tab) retrieved October 27, 2026

Possession

  1. 03

    At one time, a licensed patient can legally possess up to 3 oz. of marijuana on their person and up to 8 oz. in their residence.

    Source: oklahoma.gov (official source, opens in a new tab) retrieved October 27, 2026

  2. 04

    A licensed patient can also possess up to 1 oz. of concentrated marijuana, 72 oz. of edible marijuana, 72 oz. of topical marijuana, 6 mature plants and 6 seedling plants.

    Source: oklahoma.gov (official source, opens in a new tab) retrieved October 27, 2026

Where use is not allowed

  1. 05

    Smoking and vaping medical marijuana outdoors in public, or indoors at a workplace, is subject to the same state laws as smoking tobacco.

    Source: oklahoma.gov (official source, opens in a new tab) retrieved October 27, 2026

Driving

  1. 06

    Since November 1, 2025 (SB 518), medical marijuana labels must carry the warning: "It is illegal to drive a motor vehicle while under the influence of marijuana or marijuana products."

    Source: oklahoma.gov (official source, opens in a new tab) retrieved October 27, 2026

  2. 07

    The Oklahoma Highway Safety Office says drugged driving is still impaired driving, and that some people may be impaired for more than five hours after using marijuana.

    Source: oklahoma.gov (official source, opens in a new tab) retrieved October 27, 2026
