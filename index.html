Cannabis basics

5 questions

08 What is the difference between THC and CBD?

THC (delta-9-tetrahydrocannabinol) and CBD (cannabidiol) are the best-known cannabinoids in the cannabis plant. THC is the main intoxicating compound, the one that causes a high. CBD is not intoxicating in the way THC is. Product labels list how much of each a product contains.

09 What do indica, sativa and hybrid mean?

They are traditional names for cannabis plant types: indica plants tend to be short and bushy, sativa plants tall with narrow leaves, and hybrids are crosses of the two. Shops still use the labels to group strains, but how a product feels depends more on its cannabinoid and terpene content, and on the person, than on the label.

10 What are terpenes?

Terpenes are aromatic compounds made by many plants, cannabis included. They give each strain its scent, from citrus to pine to pepper. Common ones include myrcene, limonene and pinene, and some lab-tested products list their terpenes on the label.

11 How are edibles different from smoking?

Start low and go slow. Eaten cannabis is processed through the digestive system, so its effects come on much more slowly than smoking or vaping and can last longer. Wait until the first serving has taken full effect before having more.

12 How should I store cannabis at home?

Keep it in its original child-resistant packaging, locked away or out of reach and out of sight of children and pets. Edibles can look like ordinary sweets, so keep them apart from food. A cool, dark, dry place also keeps products fresh. If a child or pet swallows cannabis, call Poison Control at 1-800-222-1222 or your vet.

General information, not medical or legal advice. Rules differ by state and can change; check your store's page before you visit.
